## Why This Chapter Matters

L6 compensation is complex: base salary + RSU grant + sign-on bonus, each with
different vesting schedules, tax implications, and negotiation levers. Understanding
how to evaluate and negotiate an L6 offer is worth more than almost any other
single chapter in this guide — both in dollar terms and in the skills it builds
for future negotiations.

## Planned Content

### Part 1: L6 Compensation Structure
- Base salary: fixed annual cash; typically $200K–$350K at large tech companies
- RSU (Restricted Stock Units): company stock vesting over 4 years; often the largest component
  - Example: $600K RSU grant, 4-year vest = $150K/year at grant price
  - Risk: stock price can go up or down; this is not guaranteed cash
- Sign-on bonus: one-time cash payment in year 1; often used to offset unvested RSUs you're
  leaving behind at your current company
- Annual bonus: 10–20% of base; depends on company performance + individual rating
- Total Compensation (TC) = Base + RSU/year + Bonus; this is the number to optimize

### Part 2: How to Compare Offers Across Companies
- Never compare base salaries alone — TC is what matters
- RSU comparison: use current stock price, but model 3 scenarios (flat, +30%, -30%)
- Vesting schedule matters: 1-year cliff + monthly after (Google) vs. backloaded (Amazon)
  - Amazon: 5% yr1, 15% yr2, 40% yr3, 40% yr4 — terrible if you leave after year 2
  - Google/Meta: 25% per year, monthly after cliff — much more friendly
- Location adjustment: $300K TC in SF ≠ $300K TC in Austin (cost of living difference)
- Benefits: health insurance quality, 401k match, parental leave — hard to quantify but real

### Part 3: Researching the Market Rate
- Sources: levels.fyi (most accurate for tech TC), Glassdoor (less precise), Blind
- Ask your network: engineers at target company at the same level — what's their TC?
- Recruiter ranges: recruiters often have a band; their first offer is usually below midpoint
- Leverage: the more offers you have (or can credibly claim), the more you can negotiate
- The right framing: "I'm very excited about this role. I want to make this work.
  I'm currently evaluating another offer at $X TC — can you match or beat that?"

### Part 4: The Negotiation Conversation — Scripts That Work
- After receiving the offer: "Thank you — I'm very excited. Can I have a few days to review?"
  (Always buy time. Never accept on the spot.)
- Counter #1 (base): "Based on my research and the other offers I'm evaluating,
  I was expecting something closer to $X on base. Is there flexibility there?"
- Counter #2 (RSU): "The base works. The RSU grant is lower than I expected for this level.
  Can we get to $Y total?"
- Counter #3 (sign-on): "I have $Z in unvested equity I'd be leaving behind. Can you
  offset that with a sign-on bonus?"
- The key rule: always give a number. "I was expecting more" is weak. "$50K more in RSUs"
  is negotiable.

### Part 5: Competing Offers — The Strongest Lever
- A competing offer from a peer company is the single most powerful negotiation tool
- How to use it: "I have an offer from [Company X] at $Y TC. I prefer this role —
  can you get closer to that number?"
- You don't have to disclose the exact number: "significantly higher" is acceptable
- Exploding offers: "offer expires in 48 hours" is usually a bluff; ask for an extension
  ("I need more time to make an informed decision — can we extend to Friday?")
- Counter-offers from your current employer: usually a sign they undervalued you;
  think carefully before accepting (the reasons you wanted to leave haven't changed)

### Part 6: What You Can and Cannot Negotiate
- Usually negotiable: base salary, RSU grant, sign-on bonus, start date, remote work %
- Sometimes negotiable: level (rare; requires re-leveling process), reporting structure
- Usually not negotiable: vesting schedule, annual bonus %, benefits package, stock options
  (at public companies — at startups, option count and strike price are negotiable)
- The limit: companies have internal equity bands; they won't exceed band for your level
  (if you want more, the answer may be "we need to re-level you to L7")

### Part 7: Evaluating the Full Package — Beyond TC
- Job scope: are you owning a system/area you care about? (impacts career trajectory)
- Team quality: your peers accelerate or limit your growth
- Manager quality: one good manager is worth $50K/year in career acceleration
- Company trajectory: is the stock likely to appreciate? (RSU value depends on this)
- Work-life balance: 60-hour weeks → implied wage cut of 33% vs. 40-hour weeks
- Location: remote, hybrid, in-office — not just lifestyle but networking implications

### Part 8: After You Sign — The First 90 Days
- Negotiate start date to maximize departing vesting: if your RSUs vest on the 1st,
  don't start the new job until after that date
- Sign-on bonus repayment clauses: usually 1-year clawback; read the fine print
- 401k rollover: roll old 401k to new employer or IRA; don't cash out (tax + penalty)
- Stock sale timing: RSUs vest as ordinary income; selling creates capital gains;
  consult a tax advisor for large grants
