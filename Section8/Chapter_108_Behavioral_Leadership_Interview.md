# Chapter 101: Behavioral & Leadership Interview — L6 STAR Answers

> The behavioral interview is not a box-checking exercise. At L6, the interviewer
> is asking: "Does this person think and operate at Staff scope?" Every answer
> must demonstrate cross-team influence, ambiguous problem navigation, and
> measurable org-level impact — not just personal execution.

## Why This Chapter Matters

Every L6 interview loop includes behavioral rounds. Google's "Googleyness", Amazon's
14 Leadership Principles, Meta's "impact and influence" assessment — they all probe
the same thing: does this person's judgment and behavior match what we expect at
Staff/Principal level? A weak behavioral round can kill an otherwise strong loop.

## Planned Content

### Part 1: What L6 Behavioral Questions Actually Test
- Not: "were you a good teammate?"
- Yes: "did you operate at Staff scope — cross-team influence, ambiguity navigation,
  org-level trade-offs, and measurable impact?"
- The evaluation axis: scope of impact (team → org → company) + ambiguity handled +
  influence without authority + long-term thinking
- Common failure: giving L4/L5 answers to L6 questions (personal execution, not org impact)

### Part 2: The STAR Framework at L6 — What Changes
- Situation: set the scope — why did this matter at the org level?
- Task: what was unclear or contested? (L6 answers involve ambiguity)
- Action: what did YOU specifically do? (not "we" — be specific about your role)
  At L6: include how you influenced others, resolved disagreements, changed direction
- Result: quantified outcome at org level (not just "the feature shipped")
- Time per story: 3–4 minutes; practice cutting to 2 minutes for follow-ups

### Part 3: Amazon Leadership Principles — The L7 Bar
- 14 LPs: Customer Obsession, Ownership, Invent and Simplify, Are Right A Lot,
  Learn and Be Curious, Hire and Develop the Best, Insist on Highest Standards,
  Think Big, Bias for Action, Frugality, Earn Trust, Dive Deep, Disagree and Commit,
  Deliver Results
- What they're looking for at L7: examples where you applied LPs at org/company scope
- "Disagree and Commit": L6 answer = you had data, made the case, didn't win, still
  committed fully and made the project succeed despite disagreement
- "Dive Deep": L6 answer = you found a root cause others missed because you went deeper
  into the data/system than the team expected

### Part 4: Google's Googleyness + Leadership Assessment
- Googleyness: "Does this person make the people and teams around them better?"
- Leadership: "Does this person drive change at the org level without formal authority?"
- Common questions:
  - "Tell me about a time you influenced a decision you didn't control"
  - "Tell me about a time you had to change course significantly mid-project"
  - "How do you approach technical disagreements with senior engineers?"
  - "Tell me about a time you made a technical decision that turned out to be wrong"

### Part 5: Meta's Impact & Influence Framing
- Meta cares deeply about measurable impact: "how many users? what metric moved?"
- Also cares about speed: "how did you accelerate this?"
- Cross-functional influence: "who did you need to convince and how?"
- Common questions:
  - "Tell me about your most impactful project in the last year"
  - "Tell me about a time you had to influence without authority"
  - "Tell me about a time you significantly changed the direction of a project"
  - "Describe a time you had to make a decision with incomplete information"

### Part 6: Preparing Your Story Bank
- Build 6–8 strong stories that can answer most behavioral questions
- Each story should cover: technical depth + cross-team influence + measurable result
- Story categories to cover:
  - Technical decision with trade-offs (you were right or wrong — both work)
  - Cross-team conflict or misalignment (how you resolved it)
  - Ambiguous project (how you brought clarity)
  - Failed project or mistake (what you learned and what changed)
  - Mentorship or leveling up others
  - Proactive initiative you started without being asked
- Practice: tell each story in 2 minutes without notes

### Part 7: Questions to Ask Interviewers
- Behavioral interviewers often give time for your questions: use it
- Good L6 questions: "What does the first 90 days look like for this role?" /
  "What's the biggest technical challenge the team is facing?" /
  "How does this team make decisions about technical direction?" /
  "What would make someone spectacularly successful in this role?"
- Avoid: "What's the salary?" / "How many vacation days?" / "Is there remote work?"

## The One-Sentence Summary

> "L6 behavioral interviews test scope, not execution — every story must show cross-team influence, ambiguity navigation, and measurable org-level impact; build a bank of 6–8 strong stories covering technical trade-offs, conflict resolution, failed projects, and proactive initiatives, and practice delivering each in under 3 minutes with specific numbers."
